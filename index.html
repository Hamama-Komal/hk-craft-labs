02

What people usually come with

Four situations. Yours probably sounds like one of them. If it doesn't, write anyway.

  1. “I have an idea, but there's no app yet.”

    We cut the idea down to the smallest version that proves it, and I build that as a real Android app in Flutter. It goes out through the Play Console under your account, so the listing is yours.

    Flutter · Firebase · Play Store release

  2. “Our app works. Mostly.”

    Crashes you can't reproduce, screens that stutter on cheaper phones, a codebase nobody wants to touch. I read it first and write down what's wrong, then fix it in an order that keeps the app shipping.

    Audits · Clean Architecture · Performance

  3. “We want AI in it, but something actually useful.”

    Writing and grammar assistance, answers drawn from your own documents (RAG), or an LLM that handles a repetitive step in your workflow. I'll also tell you where a plain rule would do the job better than a model.

    LLMs · RAG · Python · FastAPI

  4. “I just need someone to think it through with.”

    Before you spend money, a half-hour call to test the idea: who it's for, what the first version needs, what can wait, and roughly what it takes. No obligation to hire me afterwards.

    Free first call · Honest scoping

04

How a conversation turns into a project

You can stop after any step. Steps one to three cost nothing.

  1. 1

    You write a few lines

    What's wrong, or what you want to exist. A rough paragraph is enough, and you don't need technical words.

  2. 2

    I reply with questions

    Within two working days. Usually three or four questions that change what should be built.

  3. 3

    A call, if it helps

    Thirty minutes on a call or WhatsApp. We agree on the smallest useful version, and on what can wait.

  4. 4

    A written plan

    Scope, milestones, timeline and price, in writing, before any work starts. Nothing gets added later without asking you.

  5. 5

    Builds you can install

    A new build on your phone at every milestone, so you're judging the real app and not a status report.

05

Straight answers

Is it really just you?

Yes. That's the point of the studio: the person you explain the problem to is the person who solves it. It also means I take on only a few projects at once, and I'll tell you honestly if I don't have room.

Who owns the code and the app?

You do. The source code, the Play Console listing and any accounts we set up are in your name by the end of the project.

Can I share my idea safely?

Yes. I'm happy to sign an NDA before you go into details. Until then, you can describe the idea in general terms.

What does it cost?

It depends on scope, which is why the price comes in the written plan and not before. A small fix and a new app are different jobs. If your budget is tight, say so early and we'll shape the first version around it.

Do you build for iOS?

Flutter builds for both, but Android and Google Play are where I've shipped. If you need an iOS release, we'll talk about it plainly at the start, not partway through the project.

What if I only want advice?

That's fine. A lot of good projects start with a conversation that ends in “don’t build that yet”. Write in and ask.
